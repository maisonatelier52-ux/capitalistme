GTA 6 Launches Worldwide as UK Broadband Networks Hit Record Traffic

By ISABELLE CROWLEY

November 20, 2026

Grand Theft Auto VI has finally reached players. Rockstar Games’ long-delayed sequel completed a phased worldwide release on Thursday, going live at midnight in each country and becoming available everywhere from New Zealand to the UK. It arrives 13 years after its predecessor, with critics close to unanimous in their praise and British broadband networks reporting some of the heaviest traffic they have ever carried.

The game, which runs on PlayStation 5 and Xbox consoles, is set in the fictional US state of Leonida and follows two protagonists, Lucia and Jason, as what should have been an easy score goes wrong. It carries an 18 rating in the UK for strong graphic violence, strong sexual themes and use of illegal drugs.

Critics Deliver Near-Universal Praise

Reviews published ahead of release were overwhelmingly positive. According to the BBC, review aggregator Metacritic put the game’s average score at 99 out of 100 from 150 critic reviews. Nintendo’s The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time has held the top place on the site since 1998, and the BBC reported that Metacritic confirmed the record had now officially been overtaken, although some other outlets describe the score as drawing level with Ocarina of Time’s 99.

Writing for the Guardian, Keza MacDonald gave the game five stars, praising the depth of its world and the relationship between its two leads. She said it was the first Grand Theft Auto in which she felt women were treated as people rather than props, while acknowledging that sexism is still present in the world the game depicts.

Players were quick to share first impressions once the game unlocked. Aleana Fernando in Auckland, among the first in the world to play, told BBC Newsbeat that the graphics felt impressive from the opening set-up screens. Charlie Wood in London said he had laughed at the dialogue before reaching the gameplay and said that about 30 minutes in it was “most definitely worth the wait”, while a player in Germany said the number of in-game encounters made the game feel like a life simulation.

UK Broadband Networks Hit Record Traffic

The size of the game, a 212GB download that players could fetch in advance, put unusual pressure on home internet connections. BT said the biggest broadband traffic peak ever recorded across its BT and EE networks came on Wednesday 18 November, the night before release, and described the download as equivalent to almost 80 hours of HD streaming. Luciano Oliveira, BT’s managing director of consumer products, said the game had driven a level of demand the company had never seen before.

Virgin Media O2 reported that its evening traffic between 8pm and 10pm on Wednesday was 9.4% higher than its previous record, which had itself been set a week earlier during the pre-load period. Traffic was also 13.6% above the record set earlier this year during the match between Arsenal and Atlético Madrid.

CityFibre, which describes itself as the UK’s largest independent broadband provider, said traffic rose 22% as gamers began downloading at the moment of launch, setting a new peak of 6.65 terabits per second against its previous record of 5.45.

BT and EE data showed that Fort Augustus, a village on Loch Ness, recorded the biggest percentage rise in gaming download traffic in the UK after pre-downloads began. Feltham in west London came second, followed by Liverpool, Sheffield and Birmingham. Not everyone had an easy time, however: speed tests analysed by Uswitch suggested the download could take more than two days in places such as Caistor in Lincolnshire, while the median speed across more than 825,000 UK tests implied a wait of just over six and a half hours.

BT said it had prepared by increasing caching and capacity where possible, placing copies of the game closer to customers so that downloads did not all have to travel from their original source, and putting enhanced monitoring and network controls in place to protect both fixed and mobile networks.

Fans Book Leave and Schools Push Back

The launch has spilled well beyond gaming. The Press Association reported that an estimated 2.8 million UK adults were expected to get hold of the game on launch day, with thousands booking annual leave and many more expected to call in sick. The BBC spoke to fans taking time off work, including one who planned a week completely off grid in Wales, and to a company that gave its staff the day off to play.

Some schools reminded parents and pupils to put study first, and one school in Essex offered prizes such as vouchers, Christmas dinners and a pass to skip the lunch queue to encourage attendance. Because of the 18 rating, the game can only legally be sold to adults, though the rating does not stop an adult from letting a child play; it is meant to inform that decision.

Research commissioned by EE from Censuswide, based on a survey of 1,500 UK gamers, found that 52% said the game would take priority over their romantic partner or dating life, while 36% expected to play for at least five hours a day during launch week. It also found that 96% of British gamers rate a good broadband connection as important as the console itself.

Controversy Behind the Launch

The release has not been free of controversy for Rockstar. The BBC reported that a 21-year-old from Oxford pleaded guilty on Monday to the hack of Rockstar Games and a handful of other attacks he carried out at the ages of 16 and 17 as part of the Lapsus$ hacking gang. He stole internal company data that included 90 clips showing almost an hour of gameplay from the unfinished game.

A group of sacked Rockstar workers has also been protesting outside the company’s offices ahead of an employment tribunal. The tribunal heard that the workers were penalised for discussing working conditions in a private online forum while working on the game. They say they were punished for being part of a trade union. Thomas Cordrey, representing Rockstar, said staff had made disparaging comments about the company on a semi-public Discord server and shared confidential information.

Why the Launch Matters

Rockstar North, which is headquartered in Edinburgh, delayed the release twice, saying it needed extra months to reach the level of polish that fans expect. The wait has built enormous expectations: the BBC noted that GTA V became one of the most successful entertainment products of all time, with some estimates putting its net profits as high as $10bn (£7.6bn).

For now, the evidence from critics, players and network operators points the same way. BT described the pre-download rush as the biggest gaming download event the UK has ever seen, and the game has arrived with a Metacritic score that sits at the very top of the site’s all-time list.
