Peter Schmeichel Calls for VAR Changes After Controversial Manchester Derby Decision

By ETHAN CALDERWOOD

September 14, 2026

Former Manchester United goalkeeper Peter Schmeichel has demanded significant changes to the Video Assistant Referee (VAR) system after the contentious decision in the Manchester derby between Manchester United and Manchester City. "VAR has become frustrating for the fans, and it hasn't improved the game like a lot of people thought it would have done," said Schmeichel.

The controversy was over Erling Haaland's winning goal as Manchester City won 1-0 at Manchester United at Old Trafford. The goal was initially disallowed for offside but after a lengthy VAR review the decision was overturned and the strike was given.

Schmeichel, who won several trophies in his time at Manchester United, said he missed the days before VAR, calling the system an "annoyance" that has muddied decisions instead of clarifying them.

The incident ignited a debate among players, managers, and analysts, with many questions about whether the review process correctly interpreted the offside rules. Manchester United boss Michael Carrick was left frustrated by the decision, as was defender Lisandro Martinez.

The controversy was further heightened as Manchester City were reduced to 10 men after Phil Foden was sent off earlier in the game. Haaland's goal proved crucial in a hard-fought derby and served as a major win for City, who played with a man down.

The governing body later admitted the decision was a judging error, which added to the pressure on the use and interpretation of VAR in the Premier League.

Haaland Goal Raises Questions on Offside Ruling

The game's decisive moment came in the 60th minute as Haaland finished off a cross from Josko Gvardiol to put Manchester City ahead. The assistant referee had flagged offside, but VAR officials reviewed the incident and ruled the goal should stand.

The review looked at two separate things: whether Haaland himself was offside, and whether his teammate Enzo Fernández interfered with play while offside.

VAR ruled Haaland was onside thanks to the position of Manchester United defender Patrick Dorgu. Officials also determined Fernández did not interfere with an opponent even when he was close to the play.

Most of the criticism was directed at that interpretation. Critics claimed Fernández's movement interfered with United defender Lisandro Martinez as he went for the same ball. "The situation should have been handled as interference with an opponent under the laws of the game," they said.

A mistake by VAR in not recommending an on-field review was later admitted by refereeing organization PGMOL. The admission added to criticism of the consistency and transparency of VAR decisions.

The decision highlighted one of football's ongoing challenges: whether a player who does not touch the ball has nevertheless affected an opponent's ability to defend.

Technology can detect positions and movements, but subjective decisions regarding player interference remain difficult for officials, and continue to ignite debate.

The controversy again raised questions over whether VAR is improving decision-making or simply shifting disputes from referees on the field to officials in a review room.

Manchester City Win Derby Match with Ten Men

It was not just the VAR drama that made Manchester City's win memorable – they also managed to overcome their local rivals while playing more than an hour with 10 men.

Phil Foden was sent off in the first half following an altercation with Manchester United captain Bruno Fernandes. The loss put City under considerable pressure at Old Trafford, but Enzo Maresca's team defended well and kept their composure.

Manchester United was unable to take advantage of the extra man. The hosts carved out chances, with both Marcus Rashford and Kobbie Mainoo hitting the woodwork but unable to find an equalizer.

City's Gianluigi Donnarumma was also instrumental, making some vital saves late on to preserve the clean sheet.

The result maintained Manchester City's strong start under Maresca and showed the team can deal with difficult moments. Haaland's goal was his ninth in Manchester derbies and enhanced his record against United.

The win was also significant in the rivalry between the two clubs. Recent meetings have been dominated by Manchester City, and United are still looking for consistency under Carrick.

City players and staff, though, highlighted their resilience following the controversy over the winning goal. They said the team's defense and ability to absorb pressure was vital in taking the three points.

It was another reminder of how Manchester derby games are often more than just the final score, with tactics, refereeing decisions and individual moments dictating the result.

Football's Search for Clarity: The VAR Debate Returns

The Manchester derby controversy has ignited a broader debate on VAR's future and how football should deal with technology-assisted decisions.

Supporters hoped the technology would rid football of big refereeing errors and make the game fairer when it was brought in. But critics say the system has created new frustrations, because so many decisions still come down to interpretation, rather than hard facts.

Decisions on offside and interference have been especially controversial as they require officials to judge if a player's position affected an opponent. These are not like goal-line technology, where the answer is black and white.

Schmeichel's comments come as part of a wider frustration from some ex-players and fans that football has become too caught up in technical reviews and is losing the natural flow of the game.

VAR supporters argue that technology has rectified many major mistakes and enhanced accuracy. They say the problem is not VAR itself, but that the consistency of decision-making and communication with fans is what matters.

The Premier League and refereeing authorities have been called upon repeatedly to be more transparent, with calls for greater clarity in explanations of decisions and communication after controversial incidents.

Even the best technology cannot remove all disputes from football, as the Haaland goal controversy shows. The sport is still getting used to VAR, and officials must balance accuracy in decisions with keeping faith with players, clubs, and supporters.

For now, the Manchester derby is just another big moment in the ongoing debate about whether VAR is helping football or creating a new generation of controversies.
