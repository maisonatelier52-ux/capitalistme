Russell Brand Faces Sentencing After Rape and Sexual Assault Convictions

By LUCAS HARRINGTON

November 9, 2026

Russell Brand is due to be sentenced at Southwark Crown Court in London on Monday, 9 November, after a jury found him guilty last week of raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others. Judge Joel Bennathan has already told the comedian and actor that he faces a significant prison term.

The 51-year-old was convicted on Thursday, 5 November, following more than eight hours of deliberations by the jury over two days. He was taken into custody straight after the verdicts when the judge refused his bail application, and he has remained in prison since.

The sentencing hearing is expected to include impact statements from Brand's victims, followed by legal submissions from both sides, before the judge decides how long the sentence will be. British law gives the complainants lifelong anonymity, so none of them can be named.

What the Jury Decided After a Four-Week Trial

The trial began in mid-October and dealt with seven historical charges involving six women, with the alleged offences spread across a decade from 1999 to 2009. The jury of eight men and four women convicted Brand on four counts and cleared him on the other three.

The rape conviction relates to an incident in 2009 involving a young fan, then 21, whom Brand met after one of his stand-up shows in west London. The three sexual assault convictions relate to a woman at a birthday party in a London bar in 2004, a woman at a London radio station in 2004 or 2005, and a woman who was working on the recording of an advertisement in 2009.

Jurors found Brand not guilty of two counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault. Those charges included an allegation dating back to a Labour Party conference in Bournemouth in 1999 and an alleged incident at MTV's London offices.

Brand denied all of the charges. He told the court that he led a promiscuous life as a young man but never forced anyone to do something against their will, and that his sexual encounters were consensual. Prosecutors described a very different pattern of behaviour, portraying him as a calculating and manipulative predator who used his fame to get close to women.

His defence team argued that the complainants' accounts were weakened because they only went to the police years after the events, and after a 2023 investigation by The Times, The Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches programme. Brand's barrister, Ian Winter KC, said that earlier media coverage had tainted the case. A request to have the trial thrown out on those grounds was rejected before it began, ABC News reported.

Judge Refuses Bail as Police Say More Victims May Come Forward

After the verdicts, Brand's lawyers asked for bail so that he could spend time with his three children. Judge Bennathan refused, saying there were substantial grounds to fear that Brand, who has considerable resources, might not return to face his sentence. The judge said he regretted the hardship for the children but added that it had been caused by their father's serious criminal offending.

Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy of the Metropolitan Police said the conviction was a testament to the courage and determination of the women who reported what happened to them. He said Brand had used his influence, status and fame to exploit women, and hoped the outcome sent a clear message that nobody is above the law.

Police have also confirmed that they have passed prosecutors a file on two further rape allegations against Brand. Metropolitan Police Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley said he would not be surprised if more people came forward and urged them to do so. ABC News also reported that two civil claims linked to Brand's time in the United States are under way.

Brand rose to fame in the early 2000s as a television and radio presenter and later appeared in Hollywood films. In recent years he moved away from mainstream media, relocated to Florida and built a large online following with videos about wellness, conspiracy theories and his Christian faith. He continued to post online while the trial was under way, the Associated Press and ABC News reported.

Political Reaction and Questions for the Media Industry

Prime Minister Andy Burnham described Brand's crimes as horrifying, PA Media reported. Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy said not enough had changed inside media organisations to stop powerful figures preying on women, and said she could not honestly claim that something similar could not happen today. She called for far more robust processes to protect women who come forward.

The Victims' Commissioner for England and Wales, Claire Waxman, called for a new legal duty on judges to direct juries about rape myths and misconceptions whenever they arise in a trial. Her call followed defence arguments that the complainants had not screamed, fought back or run away when they were attacked.

Mr Winter, Brand's barrister, also became part of the story after the verdicts. He referred himself to the Bar Standards Board over an angry exchange in a court corridor with Sunday Times journalist Rosamund Urwin, who worked on the original 2023 investigation.

What Happens at Sentencing

Rape carries a maximum sentence of life imprisonment in England and Wales, and the judge has made clear that the rape conviction alone will lead to a significant custodial sentence. Brand has been in custody since the verdicts, so the time he has already spent in prison is expected to be counted towards whatever term he is given.

The case is already being seen as one of the most high-profile criminal trials in recent British history, and it has put fresh attention on how powerful figures in entertainment were able to avoid scrutiny for so long.
