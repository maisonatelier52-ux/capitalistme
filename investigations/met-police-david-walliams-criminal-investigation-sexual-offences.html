Met Police Open Criminal Investigation Into David Walliams Allegations

By DANIEL WHITMORE

November 15, 2026

The Metropolitan Police have launched a criminal investigation into allegations of serious sexual offences linked to David Walliams, the comedian and children’s author, days after he was named in a joint Guardian and ITV News investigation. The force announced the inquiry on Friday 13 November 2026. In line with its policy it did not name anyone, but the Guardian and other outlets reported that it concerns Walliams, 55, who denies any wrongdoing.

Detectives from the Met’s complex sexual offences unit are leading the case. No arrest has been made, the Guardian reported that no searches have been carried out, and nobody has been charged.

What the Met Police Has Said

In its statement, the Met said it had received a number of allegations of “non-recent serious sexual offences involving a man now in his 50s” in recent days, and that those who came forward were being supported by specialist officers. Detective Superintendent Mike Cagney, who leads the investigating team, urged anyone who has been the victim of a sexual offence to come forward, however long ago it happened, and said specialist officers could answer questions and guide people through the process.

The Met asked anyone with information to call 101 and quote reference 4900/10NOV26, or to contact the complex sexual offences unit on 0207 175 2738.

Separate From the Media Reports, but Under Review

The Met stressed that the allegations it is investigating are separate from those published by the Guardian and ITV News. Detectives are, however, proactively reviewing that reporting to establish whether it points to any evidence of offences. The Guardian reported that the new allegations are understood to come from several people, that some date back to the 2000s, and that at least some of those who contacted police did so this week, giving initial accounts that were enough for the Met to open a full criminal investigation.

Police declined to say what the alleged offences were, or where and when they are said to have happened, to reduce the risk that complainants could be identified. Detectives stressed that people who report sexual offences are entitled to lifelong anonymity.

The Guardian and ITV News Investigation

The reports that preceded the police announcement were published on Tuesday 10 November, after a High Court judge refused Walliams’s attempt to obtain an injunction blocking publication. Al Jazeera reported that Mr Justice Nicklin described such an order as an exceptionally serious interference with the media’s rights, and CNN reported that Walliams did not appeal.

The reports alleged that Walliams groomed fans who were as young as 15 when they met him and harassed women in the workplace over a number of years. Two women, now in their 30s, told the Guardian they first met him at 15 and felt groomed by him. The reports also alleged that he used his position on Britain’s Got Talent and at his former publisher, HarperCollins, to pursue women, and that he had a sexual relationship with a junior member of staff there.

Walliams denies the allegations. In a letter to the journalists, he said he had at times behaved thoughtlessly towards people he had casual sexual relationships with and regretted it, but that he never abused his power. He added that he is not a sexual predator and has not broken the law, and said he was devastated that some women he met 10 to 20 years ago had expressed hurt or anger towards him. His lawyers also denied the allegations of harassment and inappropriate behaviour at work.

Fallout So Far

The consequences have come quickly. His literary agency, Independent Talent Group, said it no longer represents him. The BBC began removing programmes including Little Britain from iPlayer while it reviews all material featuring or written by him, and the Labour Party suspended his membership. ITV News reported that Downing Street called the allegations deeply troubling.

HarperCollins had already stopped publishing Walliams last year, following allegations that he harassed junior female staff, which he also denied.

What Happens Next

The Met has not said how long the investigation will take. Walliams has not been arrested or charged and, as with anyone under investigation, is presumed innocent unless a court decides otherwise. Detectives will assess the new allegations alongside the material published by the Guardian and ITV News, and any decision to charge would follow the usual process involving the Crown Prosecution Service.

Support for anyone affected by sexual abuse is available in England and Wales from Rape Crisis on 0808 500 2222.
