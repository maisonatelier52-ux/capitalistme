Houthis Advance Toward Bab el-Mandeb Strait as Yemen Conflict Raises Global Shipping Concerns

By SOFIA TRENTHAM

September 11, 2026

Iran-aligned Houthi forces seized the Yemeni town of Dhubaba, near the Bab el-Mandeb strait, a key global maritime chokepoint, sources told Reuters. The episode has shone a light on security in the Red Sea, a narrow body of water that is a crucial artery for international shipping and energy.

This follows reports of Houthi forces seizing the strategic port city of Mocha (Mokha) along Yemen's western coast. The group's seizure of territory near Mocha and Dhubaba gives it greater influence along a stretch of coastline overlooking one of the world's busiest maritime routes connecting the Red Sea with the Gulf of Aden.

The Bab el-Mandeb Strait is a strategically important shipping lane connecting Europe and Asia via the Red Sea and the Suez Canal. Any disruption to the region could impact world trade flows and increase shipping costs, putting pressure on energy markets.

The Houthis, or Ansar Allah, have been embroiled in Yemen's long civil war. Iran has provided the group with military and political support, but Tehran denies exerting direct control over the group's operations. The group has targeted regional military and commercial shipping interests in previous rounds of tensions.

The latest territorial gain comes amidst wider instability across the Middle East. Yemen's security situation is tenuous and complicated by Iran-Saudi tensions in the region and other actors globally.

The Houthi advance is a major strategic problem for Yemen's internationally recognized government. The group's advance to the coast could give it more say in sea activity and talks on Yemen's future.

The developments have also alarmed global shipping companies. A greater Houthi presence in and around Bab el-Mandeb could force ships to divert, raise security costs and potentially disrupt international trade more.

Security Threats To Red Sea Shipping Routes Growing

The Houthi push to Dhubaba and Bab el-Mandeb has raised concerns about security in the Red Sea, which is often marred by fighting and attacks on vessels. It is one of the world's most strategically important waterways, connecting key trade routes between Asia, Europe and the Middle East.

The fall of Mocha and the advance to Dhubab have pushed Houthi fighters to the brink of a site where even limited strikes or threats could upend international shipping. Analysts cautioned that control or influence of the strait could provide significant leverage over maritime traffic.

Other regional waterways have been interrupted, but the Red Sea has grown more vital to energy markets and more dependent on alternative routes. Any threat to Bab el-Mandeb would also translate into uncertainty for oil shipments, container traffic and supply chains.

Oil markets have already discounted the fears of instability in the region. Analysts have warned that a prolonged disruption of key maritime routes could push up the price of energy and add to inflationary pressures around the world.

In the past, shipping lines have re-routed to avoid security threats in the Red Sea, with some vessels taking longer routes around Africa rather than using the Suez Canal corridor. That move could result in higher transportation costs, longer delivery times for global companies.

The latest moves threaten to make it more difficult for the international community to protect freedom of navigation in the region. In the past, some countries have carried out naval operations to protect commercial shipping and counter the threat from armed groups operating in the Red Sea.

Even limited fighting in Yemen can have consequences far beyond its borders, given the strategic importance of Bab el-Mandeb. Whether the Houthi expansion threatens further maritime movement will be closely looked at by governments and shipping companies.

Yemen War: Houthis Broaden Coastal Front as Fighting Intensifies

The advance by the Houthis along Yemen's west coast is a big development in the country's long-running war. The group has sought to strengthen its territorial grip against forces aligned with the internationally recognized government of Yemen.

The Houthi group's capture of Mocha and advance toward Dhubaba is expanding the group's foothold in key coastal areas. These locations are of military importance but also because of their proximity to international shipping lanes.

Renewed fighting could reverse progress made to de-escalate violence in Yemen. The country has been mired in a humanitarian crisis, economic instability and political fragmentation for years since a conflict began more than a decade ago.

Saudi Arabia and other regional actors have closely monitored the Houthis' movements, concerned that the group's expanding territorial control could result in increased pressure on their neighbors. The Houthis have also carried out cross-border attacks outside Yemen, including with drones and missiles.

Iran is a major focus of international concern. Western governments and regional foes have accused Tehran of arming and supporting the Houthis, but Iranian officials have denied they are directing the group's military decisions.

It also is part of a broader battle for influence across the Middle East between Iran and Saudi Arabia. Today Yemen is one of many battlefields where regional rivalry is played out through local armed groups and political alignments.

The new fighting has revived fears of further displacement and deteriorating humanitarian conditions for Yemeni civilians. Aid agencies have repeatedly warned that instability is hindering the delivery of life-saving supplies and aid to vulnerable communities.

The Houthi advance is thus both a military development in the local theater and a wider geopolitical challenge to regional security.

In Rising Tensions, The World Looks to a Strategic Strait

Hence, the international response to the Houthi expansion is likely to be based heavily on maritime security and stopping further disruption to the global trade routes. The Bab el-Mandeb strait is a concern because it connects to major economic areas.

Governments with a stake in international shipping are watching to see if the Houthis try to use their coastline to limit sea traffic. Following earlier threats and attacks, security in the Red Sea has already been strengthened by countries and shipping companies.

The United States and its allies have a history of responding to threats in the region and of taking action to protect commercial shipping. Further developments will tell whether more security measures are needed.

That adds to uncertainty for global markets at a time when energy prices and supply chains are sensitive to geopolitical events. The prolonged crisis in the Bab el-Mandeb region could have implications for transport costs, fuel prices and international trade patterns.

Analysts say that even small amounts of military activity could have a huge impact because of the area's strategic importance. Bab el-Mandeb is not like most war zones. It is on one of the world's major trade routes.

It also complicates future diplomacy on Yemen's future. Any political settlement will probably depend on territorial control, security guarantees and the role of regional powers.

The world will be watching to see if the war escalates or goes into a negotiation phase with Houthi forces solidifying their positions around Dhubaba and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

The coming weeks will be crucial for the future security of Yemen's coast, one of the world's most important maritime corridors.
