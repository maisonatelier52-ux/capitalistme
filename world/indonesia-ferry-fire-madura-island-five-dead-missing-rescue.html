Indonesia Ferry Fire Leaves Five Dead and Dozens Missing After Ship Catches Fire Near Madura Island

By SOFIA TRENTHAM

August 3, 2026

A passenger ferry caught fire off the coast of Indonesia near Madura Island, killing at least five people and dozens were still missing as search and rescue teams continued their operations in the Java Sea. The KMP Mutiara Sentosa 2 was carrying 271 people when the fire broke out on its journey from Surabaya in East Java to Makassar in South Sulawesi.

Thick smoke and flames covered the ferry, sending the passengers into a panic. Nearby ships came to the rescue as passengers were ordered to don life jackets and move to safer parts of the ship. Rescue efforts were continuing, with teams searching for missing passengers in difficult sea conditions, authorities said.

Madura Island Ferry Blaze

The fire erupted on the Indonesian passenger ferry while it was sailing in waters near Madura Island, off the coast of East Java. The fire started on board the ship which was carrying passengers, crew and vehicles.

The ferry was carrying 271 people, 232 passengers and 39 crew members, officials said. The ship was also carrying 181 vehicles in its regular domestic route between Java and Sulawesi. Earlier reports indicated the fire may have started from a vehicle on the ferry's car deck but officials say the exact cause remains under investigation.

Search Operation Continues; 5 Confirmed Dead

The ferry fire left at least five confirmed dead and dozens missing as rescue teams searched waters around the ferry. Nearby vessels, including tugs and cargo ships that responded to the emergency alert, rescued survivors, officials said. Some passengers crowded on the bridge and other upper levels waiting for help, as rescue teams pulled survivors from different sections of the ship. Authorities are continuing to search for missing passengers, but bad weather and sea conditions have hampered emergency crews.

Fire on Ferry, Panic Among Passengers

Survivors described scenes of panic as flames and smoke rolled through the ship. Fire spread through the ship and passengers jumped into the sea or waited for rescue boats.

Emergency crews were trying to evacuate passengers to safety and vessels in the area were providing immediate assistance. Dozens of ships were involved in the rescue operation, with Indonesian authorities coordinating efforts in the area affected. But officials say the priority is still to find the missing passengers and make sure all the survivors get medical care.

Investigation Into Cause of Ferry Fire Underway

Indonesian authorities are investigating the cause of the ferry blaze. Investigators are looking into the possibility that a vehicle carried on the ship's lower deck might have sparked the fire.

The probe will also examine safety procedures, emergency response and whether adequate precautions were taken before the ferry set sail. Maritime safety is a big concern in Indonesia, an archipelago where ferries are an essential means of transport connecting thousands of islands.

Hard Conditions for Rescue Teams at Sea

Bad conditions hampered search and rescue teams looking for the missing passengers. The size of the incident and rough seas made it difficult to search the area.

The Indonesian Navy has also sent additional ships to assist in the rescue effort and to support emergency teams on the ground near the damaged ferry. Authorities said rescue efforts would continue until all possible survivors were found.

Maritime Safety Issues Back in Indonesia

The ferry disaster has raised new questions about safety standards in Indonesia's bustling maritime transport system. Millions of people use ferries to travel between the country's islands but previous accidents have highlighted the challenge of ensuring safety rules are adhered to and of preparing for emergencies.

Experts have repeatedly called for better emergency systems, better vessel maintenance and stronger inspections to reduce the risks involved in passenger transport at sea. The most recent incident has added pressure on authorities to review ferry safety practices and improve protections for passengers.

Search Continues as Families Await Word

Rescue teams kept working after the disaster and the families of missing passengers gathered for news. Officials worked to keep the public informed, while investigators worked to identify victims and survivors.

The communities along the ferry route have been hit by the emotional toll of the disaster with relatives still waiting to hear about missing loved ones. Search efforts continue and authorities have urged the public to rely on official information.

Indonesia Continues Emergency Response After Ferry Disaster

The ferry blaze is the latest in a string of major maritime emergencies in Indonesia in recent months, highlighting the dangers passengers face when travelling across the country's sprawling archipelago.

Rescue efforts are ongoing, and authorities are concentrating on locating the missing passengers, determining the cause of the fire, and preventing similar disasters in the future. The tragedy has brought the focus back to maritime safety and emergency preparedness, and Indonesia mourns the tragedy.
